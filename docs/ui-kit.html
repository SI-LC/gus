Gus UI kit

The test for every screen: can you tell at a glance what it is and what to do next, without stopping to work it out? These are the parts that pass it. The method is the dont-make-me-think skill.

Colour has jobs

Lime means one thing: the likely next step. Amber is a warning, red is a problem, green-as-a-dot means working. Counts, headings, filters and labels are never lime.

Buttons

A button is filled and rounded, so it reads as something to press. One lime button for each card or panel: the thing you'd most likely do next. Labels say what happens ("Mark applied", not "OK"). A link-style button is for looking something up. Something you can't take back is red.

Labels and status

Text with a dot, never a box: only buttons look like buttons. Grey is a fact, amber wants a look, red is broken, green is working. How close someone is to you is plain capitals, with a question mark when it's a guess.

Headings and empty states

A page title is the name you clicked, with a count beside it and no paragraph of instructions under it. A section heading is small grey capitals. An empty state says what's missing and offers the one thing to do.

Before a screen ships

Look at it in the browser at desktop and phone widths, light and dark, empty and full, with made-up data. Add or update a case in evals/cases/ whose judge: asks: without reading any explanation, can I tell what this is, what I can press, and what to press next?
